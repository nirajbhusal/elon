Starship finally made it. On Monday morning Texas time (evening in Nepal), Flight 14 put Ship into low Earth orbit for the first time, deployed 26 operational Starlink V3 satellites, then came home early with a controlled splashdown north of Hawaii after about three hours. Musk posted "First orbital flight of Starship successful!" as it happened. Two days later he was back in Washington for Trump's Super Intelligence lunch, signed a voluntary safety accord with other lab chiefs, and cheered Joe Gebbia's America.gov portal — which he says is powered by Grok. The Tesla Roadster outdoor demo, meant for early Friday Nepal time, was pushed two weeks for weather. And he spent much of the week roasting Delta for rejecting Starlink while United and American fight over his Wi-Fi customers.
